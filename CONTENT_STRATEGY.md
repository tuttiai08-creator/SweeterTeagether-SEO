# Content Strategy

Sweeter Teagether content should bring **event planners in Dallas / DFW** to the site, then give them a natural reason to inquire about the mobile boba / dessert cart.

Traffic without inquiry-fit is not the goal. If a piece would be equally useful to a tourist, a recipe hunter, or someone planning an event in another city, it is probably the wrong piece.

## Who we are writing for

People (or planners working for them) who are:

- Choosing venues, vendors, menus, or guest experience for a celebration
- In or serving the Dallas / DFW metro
- Early-to-mid planning (researching ideas) or late planning (comparing vendors)

They are not primarily looking for “how to make boba at home” unless that query still maps to **hiring a cart for an event**.

## Intent layers

Every topic should sit in one primary layer. Secondary overlap is fine; stuffing all layers into one article is not.

### 1. Direct commercial / service intent

The searcher already wants a vendor like Sweeter Teagether.

**Examples of the *kind* of intent (not a topic list):** boba cart for a wedding, dessert catering Dallas, mobile boba bar for a shower.

**Job of the article:** Confirm fit (events, area, what the cart is), reduce friction, point clearly to menu / services / inquiry.

**Sweeter Teagether placement:** Direct and expected. Still no invented pricing, availability, or awards.

### 2. Dallas event-planning intent

The searcher is planning a specific kind of event **in Dallas / DFW** and needs local, practical help (venues, neighborhoods, timelines, vendor categories, guest experience).

**Job of the article:** Be a genuinely useful local planning resource. The cart is one relevant guest-experience option, not the thesis of every paragraph.

**Sweeter Teagether placement:** Natural — when the article discusses food, beverage, dessert, or interactive stations.

### 3. South Asian wedding / event intent

The searcher is planning a South Asian wedding or related celebration (mehndi, sangeet, walima, shower, etc.) and needs culturally specific, useful information — not a generic “multicultural wedding” article with a keyword bolted on.

**Job of the article:** Respect ceremony structure, guest flow, food timing, and regional variation. Prefer Dallas / DFW context when the query is local.

**Sweeter Teagether placement:** Only where a mobile boba / dessert station is a plausible, welcome part of hospitality — never as a substitute for traditional food or ritual.

### 4. Broader event inspiration

The searcher wants ideas (themes, menus, guest activities, photo moments) that are **not yet** locked to Dallas or to a vendor category.

**Use sparingly.** These pieces only belong if:

- The reader is still likely to be planning a real event (not entertainment browsing), and
- There is a clean path to DFW + cart without stretching the topic.

**Sweeter Teagether placement:** Light. Inspiration first; local catering as an optional next step.

## The path to Sweeter Teagether

A successful article does three things in order:

1. **Earns the click** by matching search intent (see EDITORIAL_RULES.md).
2. **Helps the planner** enough that the page would be worth reading with the CTA removed.
3. **Offers a next step** — menu, services, or inquiry — only where it is relevant.

Do not interrupt venue advice, cultural explanation, or checklists with a hard sell. Do not hide the business either: if the topic is dessert/beverage for events, say who we are once, clearly.

## Quality bar before a topic is in-strategy

Ask:

- Would this person plausibly host or book an event in DFW in the next 12–18 months?
- Does the article create a *reason* to consider a mobile boba / dessert cart, or are we forcing a mention?
- Can we write this with verified local facts, or would we have to invent?

If any answer is no, skip or reshape the topic. Do not “make it work” with a generic CTA.

## Out of scope (for now)

- National trend roundups with no DFW hook
- Recipe / at-home boba content unless it clearly serves event hiring intent
- Unrelated Dallas lifestyle content (best tacos, apartment hunting, etc.)
- Competitor smear pieces or fabricated “best of” lists
