# Editorial Rules

These rules apply to every research note and article draft. Breaking them to “hit SEO” is a failed draft.

## Search intent first

- The article must **answer the query the searcher actually has**, not the query we wish they had.
- Open with the need (planning a shower, hiring a cart, understanding a sangeet timeline). Save the brand for where it belongs.
- If the SERP is local lists, practical guides, or vendor explainers, match that format. Do not write a brand essay when people want a checklist.

## No generic AI filler

Do not ship:

- Empty superlatives (“stunning,” “unforgettable,” “in today’s fast-paced world”)
- Advice that would apply to any city or any vendor
- Padding, synonym stuffing, or repeated CTAs
- Fake expertise (“as seasoned Dallas wedding insiders…”)

If a sentence could appear on any catering site in any city, cut or rewrite it with a specific, sourced fact or a clearly labeled general principle.

## Do not fabricate

Never invent:

- Vendor names, menus, prices, hours, awards, reviews, or “featured in”
- Quotes, testimonials, or guest counts
- Locations Sweeter Teagether serves beyond what the business has approved
- Availability, packages, or “starting at” figures
- Partnerships, venues “we always work with,” or case studies

If you do not have a source, omit the claim. Do not approximate a number to look authoritative.

## Local vendor roundups

Roundups of Dallas / DFW vendors, venues, or “best of” lists are allowed **only** with **current verification**:

- Confirm the business still operates, serves events, and matches what you wrote (using their site or another primary source).
- Record the date checked and the URL in the article template’s source / verification fields.
- If you cannot verify a listing this week (or within the research window), drop it.
- Do not scrape stale listicles and rewrite them as original research.

## Sources

Prefer, in order:

1. **Primary:** the business’s own website, official venue pages, city/county pages, Sweeter Teagether’s live site for our own facts
2. **Authoritative:** established local journalism, official tourism/city event resources, well-known cultural organizations
3. **Secondary:** only to find leads — then verify on a primary page

Do not treat another blog’s roundup as a fact. Do not cite anonymous forums as proof of a vendor’s offering.

## Facts vs. interpretation

Label clearly:

- **Verified:** sourced, dated, specific
- **General practice:** “many families…,” “often…,” with no fake statistic
- **Opinion / recommendation:** Sweeter Teagether’s suggested fit (cart for guest flow, etc.), not disguised as news

South Asian and religious practice **varies**. Prefer “this is common in X communities” + source or “confirm with the family / officiant” over absolute rules.

## Sweeter Teagether mentions

- Do not stuff the brand into unrelated paragraphs (venue parking, dress codes, invitation wording).
- Mention the cart when the section is about food, drink, dessert, guest stations, or hiring catering.
- One clear, relevant mention plus a CTA is better than five awkward insertions.
- Never imply we are the only option or that we have been selected by a venue unless that is a verified, approved fact.

## Calls to action

- Include **one relevant, natural CTA** toward menu, services, or inquiry (use the live URLs once they are confirmed in the project).
- The article must remain useful **if the CTA were removed**.
- CTA copy should match intent: “See the cart and inquire” for commercial pages; “If you want a boba/dessert station at your DFW event…” for planning guides.
- No pop-up language, fake scarcity, or “book before it’s too late” unless that is a real, approved policy.

## South Asian content

- Write for real hosts: ceremony names, multi-day flow, hospitality, dietary patterns — not “exotic” flavor text.
- Do not flatten Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, Nepali, and diaspora practices into one template.
- Use terms correctly; if unsure, research and cite or leave the term out.
- Keyword targeting is not a culture. If the only South Asian element is the title, kill the topic.

## Voice

Helpful, specific, calm. Local where it is true. Commercial without being smarmy. Accessible to first-time hosts and professional planners.

## When to stop

If a central fact (hours, address, whether a venue allows outside catering, a religious requirement) cannot be verified, **stop**, flag it in the draft, and do not guess. See AGENTS.md.
